Why we built this

Best lifter awards are supposed to answer a simple question: pound for pound, who had the most impressive day on the platform? For adult lifters, formulas like DOTS answer it well. For kids, they quietly don't — and the Youth Committee could see it in the results.

The adult formulas have two blind spots. First, they treat every lifter under 40 kg (88 lbs) as if they weighed 40 kg, because they were built from adult data and adults that small barely exist. Roughly a quarter of our youth lifters weigh less than that. Second, the sport's age adjustments stop at 14. There has never been an official answer to how much more impressive a total is from an 8-year-old than from a 13-year-old — even though the honest answer is: a lot.

We checked six years of Youth Nationals. Under the adult formula, a 13-year-old took more than half of all top-five finishes, and not one 8-year-old ever cracked a top five.

That's not a knock on the 13-year-olds — they lifted great. It means the math was never giving the youngest, smallest kids a real seat at the table.

How the Youth Score works, in plain terms

We gathered over two thousand USAPL youth results from local and state meets since 2020 and asked one question of the data: what does a typical total look like for a lifter of this sex, this age, and this size? A lifter's Youth Score is simply how their total compares to that benchmark. A score of 100 means a typical performance for their own age and bodyweight; recent Nationals best lifters land around 140 to 180. Because every lifter is measured against kids like themselves, an 8-year-old and a 13-year-old can be compared fairly — and so can a 25 kg lifter and a 90 kg lifter.

Then we tested it. We applied the formula, sight unseen, to six years of Youth Nationals results it had never been trained on. The winners it picks come from every age and every size — some years a 13-year-old, some years a 10-year-old — which is exactly what a fair handicap should produce.

We're not the first to do this

USA Weightlifting hit the same wall with its youngest athletes and, in 2025, replaced its adult-derived formula with Q-Youth — a youth-specific score built by statisticians from actual youth competition data. The Youth Score follows the same playbook, fit on USA Powerlifting's own results.

Where this stands

This is a draft. The formula is a proposal before the USAPL Youth Committee for the 2027 Youth Nationals and is not yet an official USA Powerlifting scoring method. The coefficients will be re-examined as more youth results come in, and feedback from coaches, meet directors, and parents is part of the process. The calculators here exist so anyone can check the math on real lifters — we think a formula that decides awards for kids should be one anyone can inspect.

Prepared for the USA Powerlifting Youth Committee, 2026. Formula fit on 2,156 USAPL youth full-power results (2020–2026) and validated against six years of held-out Youth Nationals. Valid for ages 8–13. Questions and feedback: mrthomasmgoodwin@gmail.com.
